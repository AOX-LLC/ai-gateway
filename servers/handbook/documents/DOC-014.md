# International shipping and customs paperwork

## Where we ship

Harborline ships to Canada, Mexico and a short list of overseas destinations held in the order system. Orders to any other country are declined at checkout. Transit takes four to eight business days to Canada and Mexico and 8 to 15 for overseas. Orders to Canada are cleared faster when the buyer supplies a customs broker in advance, which many business customers already have.

## Customs forms

Every international parcel needs a commercial invoice in three copies showing the buyer, the seller, a description of each item in plain words, the quantity, the unit value in US dollars, the country of origin and the tariff classification code. Shipments to Canada also need a customs declaration form from the carrier and the buyer's tax registration number for business orders.

## Duties and taxes

Duties and import taxes are the buyer's responsibility unless the order was sold on delivered-duty-paid terms. Quote the estimate from the order system before dispatch. If a parcel is refused because the buyer will not pay duties, the return freight is charged to the buyer. Quote duties in US dollars and keep the screenshot of the estimate with the order, since customers sometimes question the figure later.

## Restricted goods

Some products need an export licence or may not be sent at all, including certain marine electronics and flares. Check the restricted list in the order system before accepting a foreign order. Lithium batteries follow a separate procedure.

## Value limits and insurance

Declare the true value. Shipments above $2,500 need a second review by the export clerk, and anything over $5,000 is insured automatically at its full value. Keep copies of customs paperwork for six years.
