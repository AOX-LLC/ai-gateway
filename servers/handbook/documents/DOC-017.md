# Cold-chain shipments

## Which products need cold-chain

A few lines we sell, including frozen bait, fish-processing enzymes and some adhesives, must be kept cold to stay usable. Frozen goods must stay at or below 0 degrees Fahrenheit and chilled goods between 34 and 40 degrees until they reach the customer. If you are unsure whether a product is temperature sensitive, check the cold-chain flag on the product page in the order system.

## Packing frozen goods

Use an insulated shipper with a rigid outer carton. Pre-chill the goods for 12 hours. Pack with gel packs or dry ice at the weights in the packing chart; for a medium shipper that is 5 pounds of dry ice for 24 hours of transit. Place a temperature logger in the middle of the load, and a dry-ice marking on the outside. Seal the lid with tape.

## Ship days and service

Cold-chain parcels ship Monday to Wednesday only, on overnight or two-day service, so they never sit in a depot over a weekend. The customer must be told the delivery day and must be home or have cold storage ready. Do not ship cold-chain goods to residential addresses without confirmation. Customer service should confirm the delivery address is staffed on the day, since carriers hold cold parcels for only one attempt.

## When things go wrong

If a logger shows the goods rose above the limit for more than two hours, the goods are treated as spoiled. Send a replacement at our cost and ask the customer to photograph the spoiled goods. Never resell goods that failed temperature.

## Records

Keep each logger report for 12 months and attach it to the order.
