# Freight claims and carrier disputes

## Scope

This document covers pallet and truck freight, as opposed to small parcels. It deals with damaged, short or lost freight and with disputes about carrier charges. Freight claims are slower and more document-heavy than parcel claims, so keep the delivery receipt, bill of lading and photographs together in the order record from the day of delivery.

## Noting damage at delivery

The receiver must write any visible damage or shortage on the delivery receipt before the driver leaves, and photograph the pallet. Delivery receipts that read clean make a later claim very difficult. Brief all dock staff on this.

## Filing a claim

Notify the carrier in writing within 9 days of delivery, and file a formal claim with the invoice, the delivery receipt, photographs and a repair or replacement estimate within 60 days. Use the claims form in the logistics shared folder. Lost freight is declared lost 30 days after the pickup date if there is no delivery scan. A carrier may deny a claim if the pallet was not packed to its standard, so pallet wrap and corner boards must be recorded in the packing log.

## Disputed charges

If a carrier bills reweigh or reclassification fees, check the weight on our dispatch record. Dispute any adjustment above $75 in writing within 30 days of the invoice. Do not short-pay the invoice while a dispute is open unless the logistics supervisor approves.

## Escalation

Claims over $5,000 or unresolved after 45 days go to the head of logistics, who decides whether to bring in the cargo insurer.
