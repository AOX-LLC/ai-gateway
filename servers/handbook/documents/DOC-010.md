# Restocking fees

## When a fee applies

A restocking fee is charged when a customer returns a non-faulty item that they simply no longer want. The fee covers inspection, repacking and the loss of shelf value. It is deducted from the refund, not billed separately.

## Standard rates

Under the current policy the restocking fee is 10 percent of the item price. Large freight items shipped on a pallet, such as dock levellers and pallet racking, carry a higher fee of 20 percent because of handling cost. The fee is never less than $5 and never more than $250 per return.

## When the fee is waived

We do not charge a fee when the item was faulty, damaged in transit, or sent in error. A fee is also waived for the first return on a new account within 60 days of the first order. Only a customer service lead may waive a fee for any other reason, and the reason must be written in the order notes. Fee waivers for goodwill are limited to $50 per customer per year. Larger concessions need the head of customer service.

## Explaining it to customers

Tell the customer about the fee before issuing the return authorisation, quote the dollar amount and show how it was worked out. For example, on a $200 item the fee is $20 and the refund is $180 before shipping adjustments.

## Wholesale accounts

Wholesale accounts follow the rates in their signed agreement. If the agreement is silent, the standard rates above apply. All fee changes are recorded in the order notes so that finance can reconcile refunds at month end.
