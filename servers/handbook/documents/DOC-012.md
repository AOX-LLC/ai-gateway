# Return authorisation (RMA) process

## What an RMA is

A return merchandise authorisation, or RMA, is a reference number that tells the Port Calder returns bay a parcel is expected and what to do with it. No parcel is accepted without one. Staff must never issue an RMA to themselves or for personal orders, because the system logs the issuer and finance reviews the log each month.

## Issuing a number

Open the order in the customer service system and choose Create RMA. Confirm the order is within the return window, select the reason code (faulty, wrong item, damaged, changed mind), and attach any photographs the policy requires. The system generates a number in the form RMA-and-six-digits and emails the customer a prepaid label or collection instructions. Where the policy requires photographs, the number cannot be issued until at least two images are attached to the order.

## Validity

An RMA is valid for 21 days. If the parcel has not been scanned by then, the number expires and the customer must ask for a new one. Staff may extend an RMA once, for 14 days, with a note explaining why.

## At the returns bay

The bay scans the RMA, inspects the goods and records the outcome as accept, partial or reject. The refund or credit is triggered automatically on accept. Partial and reject outcomes go to customer service, who contact the customer within two business days.

## Common problems

If a customer has lost their RMA email, reissue the same number rather than creating a second one. If a parcel arrives with no number, hold it for 14 days and contact the sender using the order records.
