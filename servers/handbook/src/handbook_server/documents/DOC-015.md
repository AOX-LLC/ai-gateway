# Shipping lithium batteries

## Why batteries are regulated

Lithium cells and batteries can overheat and start fires, so carriers treat them as dangerous goods. Rules depend on the battery size, how it is packed and whether it travels by air or by ground. Our own policy is to treat every battery as regulated, including those inside tools or lights, unless the shipping lead confirms otherwise.

## Air shipments

Lithium batteries may travel by air only when they are within the carrier's size limits, which are 100 watt-hours per battery for lithium-ion and 2 grams of lithium metal per cell. Batteries packed with equipment must be on the carrier's approved list. Standalone lithium-ion batteries shipped by air are limited to a state of charge of 30 percent, and cargo aircraft only. They are never allowed in passenger aircraft cargo. Anything larger than the limits must go by ground.

## Packing and labelling

Protect terminals against short circuits with tape or caps, place each battery in inner packaging, and fill gaps so nothing moves. Mark the outer carton with the lithium battery handling label and the correct UN number, and enclose the shipping paperwork. Damaged, recalled or swollen batteries must never be shipped; set them aside and tell the safety officer.

## Who may pack them

Only staff who have completed the dangerous goods course, valid for 24 months, may pack or sign for batteries. Check your certificate before starting. Ask the shipping lead if unsure.

## International batteries

Many countries add their own limits. Check the destination rules before accepting the order. Customer service must not promise delivery dates for battery orders until the dangerous goods packer has confirmed the shipment method.
